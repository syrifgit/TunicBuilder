Sewing guide

Each badge is stated the way the regulation states it. Only the badges the rules give a fixed anchor — corps title and RCAC badge from the shoulder seam; marksmanship, the star, the appointment badge, WO–CWO rank and the lowest proficiency badge from the cuff — carry an absolute figure. Everything else is a gap from the badge it hangs off, which is what you actually measure. Work down the list in order and each row is ready to sew.

Validator

Flags, never fixes. Sleeve clearance is checked against tunic size 6436, the only size measured. Anything resting on an unmeasured or estimated dimension is called out.

What this is. Enter one cadet’s insignia and get their tunic layout: a check drawing of both sleeves and the front, plus a sewing guide giving every badge’s position in centimetres from the datum it is actually measured from. Nothing is stored and nothing leaves this page.

How to use it. Work down the rail setting rank, star level, marksmanship, proficiency badges and summer training, then the front. The drawing updates as you go. When it looks right, work down the sewing guide in order — it is sorted so that anything a later badge hangs off is already placed.

Coordinate model. One axis per sleeve: y in cm upward from the cuff bottom edge, x front-positive from the sleeve centre. The shoulder seam is y = sleeve length, so nothing in the model ever measures downward. Front/rear is converted to image-left/right once, at draw time.

Highest badged level wins. Where policy says only the highest in a category is worn, that means the highest that actually carries a badge. A level marked not_worn in the workbook is skipped and the next one down is worn — which is why an ALP cadet shows the Gold Star, and a cadet whose only fitness result is “Participated” shows nothing.

Two datum families. The corps name title, the RCAC badge and the LCpl–Sgt chevrons are measured down from the shoulder seam; the chevrons hang off the RCAC badge, so they inherit it. Everything else — marksmanship, the proficiency stack, the star, the CTC grid, the appointment badge and WO–CWO rank — is measured up from the cuff bottom. The sewing guide always quotes a badge in its own family’s terms. That matters because the distance between the two families is the sleeve length: change the tunic size and every shoulder-referenced badge moves relative to every cuff-referenced one. The taper profile was measured on size 6436 and is stretched proportionally for other lengths, which is an approximation — real grading is not linear.

Artwork is stretched to the declared box. Switch Heights to Poster-derived and any badge that looks distorted under Measured is one where the local measurement and the poster art disagree. That distortion is the conflict, drawn rather than hidden.
